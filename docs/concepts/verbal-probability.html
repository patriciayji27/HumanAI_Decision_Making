The Verbal Probability Problem

When people say "likely," they mean anywhere from 55% to 90%. This variability is enormous, well-documented, and directly relevant to AI systems that communicate uncertainty in natural language.

Referenced from: Belief Elicitation · Uncertainty Communication · AI Agents

The finding

Wallsten, Budescu, Rapoport & Zwick (1986) elicited numeric equivalents for verbal probability terms and found enormous between-person variability. "Probable" ranged from about 0.50 to 0.90. Budescu & Wallsten (1995) showed context, base rates, and outcome severity all shift interpretation of the same word.

The IPCC adopted a standardized verbal scale ("very likely" = >90%), but Budescu, Broomell & Por (2009) found readers misinterpreted the intended probability ~40% of the time even with explicit definitions. Adding numeric ranges alongside verbal terms significantly improved interpretation (Budescu et al., 2014).

Relevance to AI systems

LLMs communicate uncertainty almost exclusively through verbal hedging. The problems compound: different users assign different probabilities to the same hedge word; LLM verbal confidence does not track accuracy; and unlike the IPCC or intelligence community, LLMs follow no standardized verbal-numeric mapping.

Hybrid verbal-numeric expressions ("very likely [>90%]") are the most evidence-supported intervention. This is implementable in any LLM system and does not require model changes — only output formatting changes.
