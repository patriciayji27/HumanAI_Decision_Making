Uncertain Judgements: Eliciting Experts' Probabilities

O'Hagan, Buck, Daneshkhah, Eiser, Garthwaite, Jenkinson, Oakley & Rakow · Wiley, 2006

Belief Elicitation

The specific contribution

The SHELF (Sheffield Elicitation Framework) monograph — the most comprehensive structured protocol for eliciting probability distributions from domain experts. Synthesizes decades of expert elicitation research into a concrete procedure: define the quantity, select a suitable expert, conduct a structured interview using multiple techniques, fit a parametric distribution to elicited quantiles, and validate through consistency checks.

Key methodological recommendations

StageRecommendationRationale
BackgroundDefine the quantity precisely and give the expert feedback on their general calibrationExperts are systematically overconfident; pre-calibration reduces initial anchoring
ElicitationUse quantile-based elicitation (P25, P50, P75, P95) not probability-for-valueEasier to reason about "a value I'm 75% sure won't be exceeded" than about probability at a specific value
FittingFit a parametric family (Beta, Gamma, Normal) to the elicited quantilesForces coherence; quantiles from different parts of the distribution may imply different distributions
FeedbackShow the fitted distribution back to the expert and allow revisionExperts rarely think in distributional terms; visualization often reveals unexpected implied beliefs

Qualifications

SHELF was developed primarily for engineering risk assessment, clinical trials, and environmental modeling — domains with single well-defined quantities and identifiable domain experts. Adaptation to AI interface contexts (conversational elicitation, rapid repeated elicitation from non-experts) requires significant redesign. The structured interview requirement limits scalability to large populations.
