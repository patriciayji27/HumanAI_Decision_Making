Regret in Decision Making under Uncertainty

Bell · Operations Research, 1982

Decision Quality

The specific contribution

Proposed that decision makers anticipate and seek to minimize regret — the painful emotion of comparing the outcome of a chosen option to what would have happened under the unchosen option. Regret theory provides an alternative to prospect theory that explains preference reversals without invoking probability weighting. The key equation: utility of an outcome includes a regret/rejoice term proportional to how much better or worse it is than the foregone alternative.

When regret theory applies and doesn't

ConditionRegret theory relevance
Unchosen outcome is observableHigh — regret requires knowing what you missed
Unchosen outcome is hiddenLow — no regret without counterfactual information
Decision is repeatedHigh — prior regret shapes future choices
Outcome is irreversibleVery high — regret is intensified when you can't undo the choice

Published simultaneously with Loomes & Sugden's regret theory paper (also 1982). Bell's version emphasizes the functional form; Loomes & Sugden's version emphasizes axiomatics. Together they established regret as a serious alternative to EU and prospect theory for the regret-minimization evaluation target.

Why it matters for AI decision systems

AI systems that show users only their chosen outcome — not the outcome of the unchosen alternative — suppress the regret mechanism. Systems that show counterfactual outcomes (what would have happened if you had chosen differently) activate it. This is a design choice with predictable behavioral consequences: showing counterfactuals may increase regret-averse, conservative behavior in future decisions.
