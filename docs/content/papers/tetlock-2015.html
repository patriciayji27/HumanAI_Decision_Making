Superforecasting

Tetlock & Gardner · , 2015

Belief Elicitation

Reports the results of the Good Judgment Project (GJP), a multi-year forecasting tournament funded by IARPA (Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity). Over 20,000 volunteer forecasters made predictions on hundreds of geopolitical and economic questions (e.g., "Will North Korea test a nuclear device before January 2014?", "Will Greece leave the Eurozone before December 2015?"). Forecasts were scored using Brier scores, which reward both calibration (stated probabilities match observed frequencies) and resolution (the ability to distinguish events that occur from those that don't).

The key finding: a small subset of forecasters — roughly the top 2%, labeled "superforecasters" — consistently outperformed the average by a wide margin and even outperformed intelligence analysts with access to classified information. Their Brier scores were roughly 30% better than the control group, and this advantage persisted across years, indicating it reflects skill rather than luck.

The superforecasters' cognitive strategies were identified through surveys and interviews: (1) Break questions into sub-components and estimate each separately (Fermi decomposition). (2) Start from base rates (the outside view) before adjusting for case-specific information. (3) Update incrementally — small, frequent adjustments rather than large swings. (4) Use granular probabilities (distinguishing 65% from 70%) rather than round numbers. (5) Actively seek disconfirming evidence and weight it appropriately. (6) Collaborate — the best teams outperformed the best individuals, and extremizing aggregation (pushing group averages away from 50%) improved predictions further.

A training intervention based on these strategies improved forecasting performance among regular participants, demonstrating that calibration is partly a teachable skill, not just a talent. The improvement was domain-specific, however — good performance on geopolitical forecasting did not transfer automatically to other domains (medical prediction, technological forecasting).
