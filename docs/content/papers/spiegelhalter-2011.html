Visualizing Uncertainty About the Future

Spiegelhalter, Pearson & Short · Science, 2011

Uncertainty Communication

The specific contribution

Influential Science review synthesizing visualization approaches for communicating future uncertainty across demographics, climate science, and finance. Argues that false precision — showing point estimates without uncertainty — is systematically misleading and that conveying uncertainty, even imperfectly, is better than false confidence. Proposes a taxonomy of uncertainty communication problems and matches visualization types to each.

Core taxonomy

Uncertainty typeRecommended displayKnown failure mode
Single binary outcome probabilityIcon arrays, natural frequenciesDenominator neglect with small icons; numeracy effects
Continuous forecast distributionFan charts, interval bandsCI misinterpretation; deterministic construal error
Multiple plausible scenariosEnsemble spaghetti plotsFrequency-as-probability confusion in ensembles
Long-horizon uncertaintyWidening interval bands over timeUsers anchor on the near-term, not the long-term spread

Qualifications

Largely normative and conceptual — proposes what displays should communicate rather than testing what they actually produce behaviorally. The empirical literature on which display actually improves decisions was thin at publication and has partially qualified the recommendations since. The comprehension-use gap (Hofman et al. 2020) in particular challenges the assumption that more accurate uncertainty display leads to better decisions.
