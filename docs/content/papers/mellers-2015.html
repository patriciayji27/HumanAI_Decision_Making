Prediction and Judgment: Why Superforecasting Works

Mellers, Stone, Atanasov et al. · , 2015

Belief Elicitation

Formal analysis of the Good Judgment Project results, published in Psychological Science. Reports two specific mechanisms that improved group forecasting beyond individual superforecaster performance:

Extremizing: When multiple independent forecasters cluster around a probability (e.g., five forecasters all estimate ~70%), the aggregated probability should be pushed further from 50% (toward ~85%). The logic: if multiple independent sources of evidence all point in the same direction, the strength of the combined evidence exceeds the average individual estimate. The optimal degree of extremizing depends on the correlation between forecasters — the less correlated their information sources, the more extreme the aggregate should be.

Individual-level calibration and resolution as stable traits: The study confirmed that Brier score components — calibration (do stated probabilities match frequencies?) and resolution (do probabilities vary appropriately across questions?) — predict year-over-year forecasting performance at the individual level. Forecasters who were well-calibrated in year 1 tended to be well-calibrated in year 2, and similarly for resolution. This supports treating forecasting skill as a partially stable individual difference that can be selected for (as in superforecaster identification) and trained (as in the GJP training modules).

The paper also identified a "wisdom of the inner crowd" effect: individual forecasters who made predictions on multiple occasions and then averaged their own estimates produced better predictions than their single best estimate, suggesting that within-person variability contains useful information.
