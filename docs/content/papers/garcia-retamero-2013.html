Visual Aids and Medical Risk Communication

Garcia-Retamero & Cokely · , 2013

Uncertainty Communication

Reviewed the evidence on visual aids for medical risk communication, focusing on icon arrays (also called pictographs) — displays of 100 person-figures where a subset are colored to represent a probability. For example, a 5% risk is shown as 5 colored figures among 100.

Icon arrays substantially improved risk understanding across studies, with the largest benefit for low-numeracy participants. Low-numeracy individuals (roughly 30-50% of the general population on standard measures) struggle with numeric probability formats but can process icon arrays effectively because the format converts abstract percentages into countable, visible proportions. The effect size was larger for low-numeracy participants than for high-numeracy participants, meaning icon arrays partially close the numeracy gap in risk comprehension.

Icon arrays also reduce denominator neglect — the tendency to focus on the numerator (5 people affected) while ignoring the denominator (out of 100). By making the denominator visually present (all 100 figures are visible), icon arrays force the viewer to see the affected proportion in context.

The limitation: icon arrays work well for single binary outcomes (risk/no risk) at a single probability level. They don't naturally represent distributions (how uncertain the probability itself is), comparisons between multiple uncertain options (comparing treatment A vs. treatment B), or continuous outcomes (the range of possible tumor sizes). For these more complex uncertainty communication tasks, other formats (quantile dotplots, HOPs) are needed.
