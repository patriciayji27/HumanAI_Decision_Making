Looming Large or Seeming Small? Loss Attitudes in a Representative Sample

Chapman, Snowberg, Wang & Camerer · , 2025

Decision Quality

Used a novel elicitation method called DOSE (Dynamic Optimization for Sequential Experimentation) to measure loss aversion in three nationally representative samples in the UK. DOSE adaptively selects gambles to maximize the information gained about each participant's prospect theory parameters, producing more precise individual-level estimates than standard methods.

The key finding: 47-57% of participants across the three samples were loss-tolerant (λ ≤ 1), meaning they did not weigh losses more heavily than gains. This contrasts sharply with student-sample studies, where typically 70-80% of participants show loss aversion. When the same DOSE method was applied to student samples, the results looked more conventional — suggesting the difference is about the population, not the method.

Individual loss aversion parameters elicited by DOSE were as stable over a 7-month retest interval as DOSE-elicited risk aversion and discounting measures, and more stable than traditional risk aversion measures. This suggests that wherever λ falls for a given individual, it is a relatively stable preference parameter rather than a noisy context-dependent response. The study was conducted with an online panel (N ≈ 2,000 in the main sample) where participants earned points exchangeable for prizes.
