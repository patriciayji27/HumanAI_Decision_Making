Strong Canonical

Subjective Probability and Expected Utility without Additivity

Schmeidler · Econometrica, 1989

Risk & Ambiguity

The specific contribution

Choquet Expected Utility (CEU) theory: a generalization of expected utility theory that allows beliefs to be non-additive (the probability of A and B need not sum to 1 when separated, unlike standard probability theory). Using a Choquet integral instead of the standard integral, CEU can accommodate ambiguity aversion — the tendency to prefer known risk over unknown uncertainty — through a convex capacity (a non-additive measure that underweights uncertain events).

How it fits with Ellsberg and Gilboa-Schmeidler

The Ellsberg paradox demonstrated that ambiguity aversion exists. Gilboa & Schmeidler (1989) proposed max-min expected utility as a normative response — maximize the minimum expected utility over a set of priors. Schmeidler's CEU (developed independently and contemporaneously) provides a representation of ambiguity aversion through a single non-additive capacity rather than a set of priors, and is formally related to the Gilboa-Schmeidler model under certain conditions.

Qualifications

Mathematically elegant but difficult to estimate empirically — the non-additive capacity has many degrees of freedom and is not directly identifiable from choice data without strong parametric assumptions. Used primarily in theoretical and macroeconomic applications; behavioral testing has been limited.
