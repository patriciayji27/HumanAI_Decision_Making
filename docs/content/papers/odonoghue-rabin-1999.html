Doing It Now or Later

O'Donoghue & Rabin · American Economic Review, 1999

Temporal Dynamics

The specific contribution

Formalized present-biased preferences (β-δ discounting) in a tractable economic model and, critically, distinguished between sophisticated and naïve agents with present bias. Standard β-δ models discount everything between now and the next period by β < 1 (present bias), then discount subsequent periods exponentially by δ. The sophistication distinction asks: does the agent know about their own present bias?

Agent typeKnows about bias?BehaviorConsequences
SophisticatedYes — correctly predicts own future choicesUses commitment devices; may anticipate that future-self will be temptedCan improve welfare through pre-commitment (deadlines, savings locks)
NaïveNo — believes future-self will have standard time preferencesPlans to do unpleasant tasks later; repeatedly puts them offChronic procrastination; never actually completes plans
Partially naiveSomewhat — underestimates own future biasMakes plans that future-self partially overridesSome commitment but insufficient to fully overcome bias

Design implications for AI

AI assistants that nudge users toward long-term goals (savings apps, health trackers, deadline reminders) are essentially providing commitment devices for present-biased agents. The sophistication question matters for design: a sophisticated user knows they need commitment and will use it willingly; a naïve user may not recognize the need and must be offered commitment through default settings or friction.
