Graphical Distribution Builders for Non-Expert Elicitation

Goldstein & Rothschild · , 2014

Belief Elicitation

Demonstrated that non-experts can construct full subjective probability distributions using interactive graphical interfaces — specifically, a bin-filling approach where participants allocate probability mass across outcome bins by adjusting bar heights. Prior to this work, full distribution elicitation was considered feasible only for trained statisticians; most practical elicitation used at most three quantiles (P10, P50, P90).

In a series of experiments, participants constructed distributions for uncertain quantities (e.g., future stock prices, sports outcomes). The constructed distributions were internally consistent: they satisfied stochastic dominance (higher values assigned higher cumulative probability), summed to approximately 1.0, and updated appropriately when participants received new information. The distributions captured meaningful information beyond point estimates — participants who knew more about a topic produced tighter distributions, and the distributional shape (skewness, kurtosis) reflected known features of the underlying data-generating processes.

The interface design mattered substantially: performance depended on the number of bins (too few produced coarse distributions; too many overwhelmed participants), the default starting shape (uniform vs. normal default shifted the final distributions through anchoring), and whether the interface displayed probability density or cumulative probability (density was more intuitive for most participants). These design dependencies mean that distribution elicitation is feasible but not design-neutral — the interface parameters are part of the measurement instrument.
