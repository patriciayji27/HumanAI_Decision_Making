The Principles and Limits of Algorithm-in-the-Loop Decision Making

Green & Chen · , 2019

AI Agents

Tested the effect of AI recommendations on human decisions in a high-stakes context: pretrial risk assessment (predicting whether a defendant will re-offend). Participants acted as judges, viewing defendant profiles and making predictions about recidivism risk.

The key finding is an asymmetric effect across correct and incorrect AI predictions: AI advice improved average decision accuracy (participants made fewer errors overall with AI assistance). However, on cases where the AI was wrong, participants performed worse than they would have without AI assistance — they anchored on the AI's incorrect prediction and failed to override it. The net improvement in aggregate accuracy masked a case-level harm on the hardest, most ambiguous cases where the AI erred.

The study revealed that AI recommendations don't just add information — they restructure how the human processes the case. Users who saw the AI's prediction spent less time reviewing the defendant's profile and were less likely to identify countervailing factors. The AI recommendation functioned as an anchor that narrowed the human's information search, not just as an additional data point in a broader evaluation.

The pretrial risk assessment context adds ethical complexity: these are decisions with significant consequences for defendants, and a system that improves average accuracy while worsening outcomes for the hardest cases may exacerbate inequities if the hard cases cluster in specific demographic groups.
