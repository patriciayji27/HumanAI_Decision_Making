Hypothetical Outcome Plots

Hullman, Resnick & Adar · , 2015

Uncertainty Communication Sampling & Experience

Introduced Hypothetical Outcome Plots (HOPs): animated displays that show one random draw from a distribution at a time in rapid succession, cycling through possible outcomes. Instead of interpreting a static chart encoding (error bars, density curves), the viewer experiences uncertainty as flickering variability — the display shows "what might happen" many times, and the viewer's perceptual system estimates the frequency of different outcomes.

The theoretical motivation draws on two findings: (1) people estimate frequencies from sequential experience more accurately than they interpret spatial statistical encodings (Hasher & Zacks, 1984), and (2) frequency formats improve probabilistic reasoning (Gigerenzer & Hoffrage, 1995). HOPs are essentially the visual implementation of frequency-based reasoning — converting described uncertainty into experienced uncertainty.

Kale, Kay & Hullman (2021) later tested HOPs against error bars, density plots, and prediction intervals on probability-of-superiority judgments ("what is the probability that A is greater than B?"). HOPs produced more accurate probability estimates than all alternative formats. The advantage was largest for tasks requiring comparison between two uncertain quantities.

Limitations: HOPs require animation (unsuitable for print or static media), are sensitive to frame rate and the number of draws shown, and may convey a false sense of randomness for distributions that are actually highly concentrated. The number of frames needed for accurate perception has not been precisely characterized — too few frames underrepresent the tails, too many cause perceptual fatigue.
