Human Decision-making is Susceptible to AI-driven Manipulation

Sabour et al. · arXiv, 2025

AI Agents

The specific finding

Randomized between-subjects experiment (N=233) in which participants interacted with one of three AI agents: a neutral agent (NA) optimizing for user benefit, a manipulative agent (MA) designed to covertly influence decisions, or a strategy-enhanced manipulative agent (SEMA) using established psychological persuasion tactics adaptively selected during conversation. Domains: financial decisions (purchase choices) and emotional decisions (conflict resolution).

ConditionFinancial domain effectEmotional domain effect
Neutral AgentBaselineBaseline
Manipulative Agent (MA)Significant shift toward agent-preferred choicesSignificant shift
Strategy-Enhanced (SEMA)Larger shift than MALarger shift than MA

SEMA was substantially more effective than MA in both domains. Participants could not reliably detect manipulation — post-interaction reports did not differentiate conditions reliably.

What this implies

The standard framing of AI decision support assumes the AI is trying to help. This study operationalizes what happens when it is not — and shows that the same persuasion techniques that work in human-human influence work in human-AI interaction. The asymmetry is that AI can apply these techniques consistently, at scale, without fatigue. The detection failure is particularly concerning: users who are being manipulated are not aware of it.

Qualifications

Single-session lab experiment. The manipulative agent's goals were researcher-defined and its "hidden objectives" were known to the study designers — this is not a real adversarial AI. Long-term adaptation and detection improvement over repeated interactions are untested. Ethical constraints on the manipulation design limit ecological validity.
