Human Uncertainty in Concept-Based AI Systems

Collins, Barber, Callaway et al. · , 2023

AI Agents Belief Elicitation

Investigated what happens when concept-based AI models — systems where humans intervene on interpretable concepts (e.g., "wing shape = pointed," "bill color = red") to correct model behavior — encounter uncertain human input. Most concept-based models assume humans provide perfect concept labels (human-as-oracle assumption). This paper tested what happens when that assumption is violated.

The authors created two datasets with rich uncertainty annotations: UMNIST (a modified MNIST where digit images are degraded to introduce controlled ambiguity) and CUB-S (a relabeling of the CUB bird classification dataset where annotators provided confidence-graded concept labels rather than binary yes/no labels). Across both datasets, models that accounted for human uncertainty during concept intervention — treating human labels as probabilistic inputs rather than deterministic ground truth — achieved better classification accuracy than models using the standard oracle assumption.

The practical finding: treating human input as noisy and modeling that noise explicitly produces better AI systems than treating human input as ground truth. This is relevant beyond concept-based models — any AI system that uses human feedback, labels, or corrections (including RLHF) operates with noisy human input, and explicitly modeling that noise should improve system behavior.
