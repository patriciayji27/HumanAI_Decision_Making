Salience and Consumer Choice

Bordalo, Gennaioli & Shleifer · Journal of Political Economy, 2013

Attention & Salience Decision Quality

The specific contribution

Extension of the authors' salience theory (Bordalo et al. 2012) to consumer markets. In a choice set, a product attribute is salient if it stands out relative to the average attribute value across options — and salient attributes are overweighted in the consumer's evaluation. The model predicts systematic asymmetries: when quality differences are large, quality is salient and overweighted relative to price; when price differences are large, price becomes salient.

Key predictions

ConditionSalient attributePredicted behavior
High-quality option stands outQualityConsumers pay a premium beyond what utility models predict
Low-price option stands outPriceConsumers are price-sensitive beyond what utility models predict
Options are similar on both dimensionsNeither (no overweighting)Choices approach expected utility predictions

Explains supermarket choice anomalies: why consumers buy expensive impulse items (quality salient) but comparison-shop for commodities (price salient). Also explains why adding a clearly inferior option to a choice set can shift demand toward the option that looks best relative to the distractor — the decoy effect, explained by salience.

Qualifications

Theoretical model fitted to stylized evidence. Distinguishing salience effects from reference-dependence and anchoring in field data is difficult. The salience index (how much an attribute value deviates from the set average) is not directly measurable from choice data alone — identification requires experimental variation.
