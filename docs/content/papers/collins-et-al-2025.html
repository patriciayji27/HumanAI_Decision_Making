Measuring and Mitigating Overreliance on LLMs

Collins et al. · , 2025

AI Agents

Position paper arguing that the harms of overreliance on LLMs extend beyond immediate decision errors to long-term consequences that current measurement approaches do not capture:

Deskilling: Users who routinely delegate cognitive tasks to AI may lose the ability to perform those tasks independently. A writer who uses LLMs for all drafting may lose writing fluency; a programmer who uses code completion for all code may lose the ability to write code from scratch. The mechanism is analogous to muscle atrophy — unused cognitive skills deteriorate.

Erosion of critical thinking: Users who habitually accept AI output without evaluation may reduce their general capacity for critical evaluation — not just for AI-related tasks, but as a transferable cognitive skill. If people practice accepting answers without scrutiny in AI interactions (which constitute an increasing fraction of daily information processing), this habit may transfer to other contexts.

Emotional dependence: Users who form parasocial relationships with AI assistants may reduce their engagement with human social support systems, with consequences for well-being and resilience.

The paper argues that current measurement approaches — typically single-session accuracy comparisons between AI-assisted and unassisted groups — cannot capture these long-term effects. Longitudinal study designs that track cognitive skill, critical thinking, and social engagement over months or years of AI use are needed but are essentially absent from the literature. These claims are theoretically motivated and plausible but empirically untested.
