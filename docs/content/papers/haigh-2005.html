Myopic Loss Aversion Among Professional Traders

Haigh & List · , 2005

Temporal Dynamics

Replicated Gneezy & Potters (1997) with professional traders from the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT). The question: does financial expertise and daily experience with risk eliminate myopic loss aversion?

The answer: no. Professional traders showed the same pattern as student participants — they invested less when they received feedback every round compared to every three rounds. In fact, the traders exhibited more myopic loss aversion than the student comparison group in this study, though the authors note this comparison should be interpreted cautiously due to small samples and potential selection effects.

The finding is important because it rules out a plausible defense of the status quo in financial interface design: "professional users understand the data and won't be affected by evaluation frequency." The evidence says otherwise — even people who trade for a living, who have extensive experience processing financial outcomes, and who intellectually understand that short-term losses are normal in positive-EV strategies, still modify their behavior based on how frequently they see the results. Sample size was small (professional traders are hard to recruit), limiting confidence in the precise effect magnitude.
