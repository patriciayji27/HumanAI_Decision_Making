Maxmin Expected Utility with Non-Unique Prior

Gilboa & Schmeidler · , 1989

Risk & Ambiguity

Axiomatized maxmin expected utility (MEU) — a decision criterion for situations where the decision-maker faces ambiguity (doesn't know which probability distribution governs the outcomes). The model: the agent has a set of possible probability distributions (priors) rather than a single prior, and evaluates each act by the minimum expected utility across all priors in the set.

Formally: the utility of act f is minp∈C Ep[u(f)], where C is the set of priors the agent considers possible. This means the agent always evaluates actions by their worst-case scenario — the prior distribution that makes the action look worst.

The model is axiomatically derived from a weakening of Savage's axioms: it replaces Savage's sure-thing principle (P2) with uncertainty aversion (if you're indifferent between f and g, you weakly prefer any mixture αf + (1−α)g over either alone — diversification is good when you're unsure of the probabilities). This axiom directly captures the Ellsberg intuition: when you don't know the probabilities, you prefer to hedge.

MEU predicts extreme conservatism: any ambiguous option is evaluated by its worst possible interpretation. This matches behavior in Ellsberg-type experiments but predicts more aversion than is typically observed in moderate-ambiguity settings. Extensions like smooth ambiguity preferences (Klibanoff et al., 2005) allow for varying degrees of ambiguity aversion rather than the binary worst-case evaluation of MEU.
