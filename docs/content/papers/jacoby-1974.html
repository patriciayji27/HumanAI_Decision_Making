Brand Choice Behavior as a Function of Information Load

Jacoby, Speller & Kohn · Journal of Marketing Research, 1974

Attention & Salience Uncertainty Communication

The specific contribution

The foundational paper establishing "information overload": when the amount of information available to a decision maker exceeds their processing capacity, decision quality and satisfaction decrease. Participants choosing between brands of rice and laundry detergent showed an inverted-U relationship between number of attributes shown and decision quality — more information helped up to a point, then hurt.

The finding and its controversy

Jacoby et al. found that subjective confidence increased monotonically with information, even as objective decision quality declined after roughly 4–8 attributes. This dissociation — people feel better informed but make worse choices — is the core overload finding. Subsequent replications produced mixed results: Malhotra (1982) found overload with 10–15 alternatives; other studies found no overload with comparable information loads.

Design implication

For AI decision interfaces: more information is not always better. The optimal display shows the attributes most diagnostic for the specific decision, not all available attributes. Explanation features that surface many features simultaneously risk triggering overload — compressing explanation to the one or two most decision-relevant factors is supported by information overload theory.
