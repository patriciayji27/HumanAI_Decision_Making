An Experiment on Risk Taking and Evaluation Periods

Gneezy & Potters · , 1997

Temporal Dynamics

Experimental test of myopic loss aversion (Benartzi & Thaler, 1995). N=72 university students. Each participant played 9 rounds of an identical gamble: invest any fraction of a 200-cent endowment in a risky option with a 2/3 chance of losing the investment and a 1/3 chance of winning 2.5× the investment. The gamble had positive expected value (EV = +16.7% per round), so a risk-neutral or moderately risk-averse agent should invest heavily.

The key manipulation: one group (HIGH feedback) saw the outcome of each round after every round. The other group (LOW feedback) saw accumulated results after every three rounds. Same gamble, same total information, different timing of feedback.

Results: the HIGH feedback group invested 50.8% of their endowment on average; the LOW feedback group invested 67.3% — a 33% increase in risk-taking from a pure information-timing manipulation. The effect was driven by the HIGH group seeing more frequent losses: in any single round, there is a 2/3 chance of loss, which is experienced as a loss event by a loss-averse agent. Over three rounds, the probability of a net loss is lower (because the positive expected value begins to dominate), making the accumulated outcome feel less threatening.

The experiment is notable for its clean design: nothing changes between conditions except when the participant receives feedback. The same gamble, the same outcomes, the same total information — only the temporal resolution of feedback differs. This isolates myopic loss aversion as the mechanism, ruling out alternative explanations based on information content.
