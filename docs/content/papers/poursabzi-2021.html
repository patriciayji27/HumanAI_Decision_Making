Manipulating and Measuring Model Interpretability

Poursabzi-Sangdeh, Goldstein, Hofman, Vaughan & Wallach · CHI 2021

AI Agents

The specific contribution

N=3,800, five pre-registered experiments. Participants were shown functionally identical house price prediction models that varied in two interpretability factors: number of features (few vs. many) and model transparency (clear internal structure vs. black-box). The study directly tested whether interpretability produces its intended effects: helping users follow the model when it is correct and detect errors when it is wrong.

The null result

Simpler, more interpretable models did not reliably help participants follow the model when it was correct or detect errors when it was wrong. More interpretable models did cause participants to switch their answers at a higher rate (they engaged more), but this did not translate into improved joint accuracy. The interpretability manipulations had smaller-than-expected effects on decision quality.

Qualifications and debate

The null result is real and important, but may be specific to how interpretability was operationalized (feature count, model transparency in a regression context). It does not rule out that better-designed explanations — ones that specifically help users identify model failure modes — could succeed. The result argues against assuming interpretability works; it does not demonstrate that no form of XAI can help. Heavily cited as a caution against interpretability hype; equally cited by critics as underspecifying the intervention.
