Anomalies in Intertemporal Choice: Evidence and an Interpretation

Loewenstein & Prelec · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 1992

Temporal Dynamics

The specific contribution

Documented four systematic violations of exponential discounting and provided a generalized hyperbolic discounting function that accommodates all of them. The violations:

AnomalyWhat it meansExample
Hyperbolic ratesImplicit discount rates are higher for near-term delays than far-term$10 now vs. $11 tomorrow implies 3650% annual rate; $10 in year 10 vs. $11 in year 11 implies much less
Absolute magnitude effectLarger amounts are discounted less per periodPeople are more patient for $100 than for $10 at the same delay
Delay/speedup asymmetryDelaying receipt requires more compensation than speeding up requires sacrificeAdding 1 month of delay requires more premium than subtracting 1 month allows discount
Sign effectGains are discounted more than lossesPeople prefer to pay bills sooner but receive rewards later (relative to what standard discounting predicts)

The generalized hyperbolic function

Loewenstein & Prelec proposed D(t) = (1 + αt)−β/α which generalizes to standard hyperbolic (β=α) and exponential (α→0) as special cases. Parameters α and β independently control the degree of hyperbolic curvature and the level of impatience, allowing the model to fit all four anomalies simultaneously.

Qualifications

Most evidence from hypothetical monetary choices. Incentive-compatible studies find smaller but qualitatively similar effects. The sign effect, in particular, is sensitive to framing and may partially reflect credit market confounds (people pay debts early because borrowing is expensive, not because of asymmetric discounting).
