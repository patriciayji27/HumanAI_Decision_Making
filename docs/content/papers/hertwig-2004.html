Decisions From Experience and the Effect of Rare Events

Hertwig, Barron, Weber & Erev · , 2004

Sampling & Experience Uncertainty Communication

The specific finding

Described probabilities and experienced probabilities produce opposite rare-event behavior:

SourceRare eventsBehaviorWhy
Description ("10% chance")OverweightedBuy lottery tickets, buy insuranceπ(0.10)≈0.18 per PT
Experience (sampling)UnderweightedIgnore tail risksWith 15 samples, 21% chance of never seeing a 10% event

The gap reversed the modal choice in 4 of 6 problems. Median sample size was 15 draws per option. Fox & Hadar (2006) showed much of the gap is sampling error (people didn't encounter the event), but a residual gap persists after controlling for sample information (Camilleri & Newell, 2011).

For AI: a user who has used an AI 20 times without a catastrophic failure has a ~36% chance of never having encountered a 5% failure rate. Their trust is rationally calibrated to their experience — but their experience undersamples the tail.
