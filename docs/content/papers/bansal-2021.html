Human-AI Complementarity in AI-Advised Decision Making

Bansal, Wu, Zhou et al. · , 2021

AI Agents Attention & Salience

The specific finding

Human-AI teams did not outperform AI alone. Humans accepted AI predictions at ~80% even when the AI was wrong and the human would have been correct independently. LIME explanations did not fix this — they increased agreement uniformly (correct and incorrect predictions alike).

The bottleneck is error detection. Complementarity requires the human to identify when the AI is likely wrong. Participants could not do this even with explanations.

The meta-analytic update

Vaccaro et al. (2024, 106 studies) confirmed complementarity is the exception. The main moderators: moderate AI accuracy (not too high, not too low) and high task difficulty. Fok & Weld (2023) found that only verifiable explanations — where the user can check the reasoning — occasionally improve complementarity.
