Strong Canonical

On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks

Guo, Pleiss, Sun & Weinberger · ICML 2017

Belief Elicitation AI Agents

The specific contribution

Documented that modern deep neural networks are poorly calibrated despite high accuracy. A model is well-calibrated if its predicted confidence matches its empirical accuracy — when the model says 90%, it should be correct 90% of the time. Guo et al. showed that deep ResNets and DenseNets trained on CIFAR and ImageNet are systematically overconfident, especially as model capacity and depth increase.

The calibration failure and the fix

MethodEffect on calibrationEffect on accuracy
Uncalibrated deep networkBadly overconfident (ECE ~5-10%)High
Temperature scaling (T>1)Substantially improved calibrationUnchanged (same argmax)
Platt scalingSimilar to temperature scalingUnchanged
Isotonic regressionBest on validation, but overfits on small setsUnchanged

Temperature scaling is a single-parameter post-hoc recalibration: divide the logits by T before softmax. T > 1 makes the distribution softer (less confident); T < 1 makes it sharper. The optimal T is fit on a validation set. It costs nothing at inference time and preserves accuracy perfectly.

Why it matters for this archive

AI confidence scores displayed to human decision makers are only useful if they are calibrated — a model that reports 90% confidence while being correct 70% of the time will systematically mislead users toward overreliance. Guo et al. established that calibration failure is the default for modern deep learning, not an edge case. Temperature scaling is now a standard post-processing step. The problem is more complex for LLMs, where calibration varies by domain, question type, and prompting strategy.
