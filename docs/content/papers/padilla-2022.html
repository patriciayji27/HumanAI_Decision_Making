Uncertainty Visualization: A Cognitive Framework

Padilla, Kay & Hullman · , 2022

Uncertainty Communication

A cognitive science framework connecting uncertainty display design choices to specific perceptual and cognitive mechanisms: preattentive processing (what the visual system detects automatically, without conscious effort), ensemble coding (how people estimate statistical properties — mean, variance — of visual sets), and mental simulation (how people reason about possible outcomes by mentally "running" scenarios).

The framework explains why certain displays work: quantile dotplots succeed because they leverage ensemble coding — people are naturally good at estimating the proportion of dots above or below a threshold without counting. Hypothetical outcome plots (HOPs) succeed because they convert statistical reading into frequency estimation, leveraging the cognitive advantage of processing sequential experience documented by Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995). Error bars fail for non-experts because they require symbolic interpretation of a summary statistic, which recruits deliberate analytical processing rather than perceptual mechanisms.

The framework provides principled guidance for selecting and designing uncertainty displays: match the display encoding to the cognitive mechanism that best supports the user's task. If the task is comparison (is A better than B?), use encodings that support ensemble comparison. If the task is estimation (what is the probability of X?), use encodings that support frequency estimation. Published in Psychological Science in the Public Interest, it serves as a review and synthesis rather than reporting new empirical results, and many of its specific recommendations await direct experimental validation.
