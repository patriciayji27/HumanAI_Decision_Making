Three Challenges for AI-Assisted Decision-Making

Steyvers & Kumar · , 2024

AI Agents

Review paper identifying three core challenges for AI-assisted decision-making, synthesizing the current state of research on each:

1. Appropriate reliance. The field has established that both overreliance (automation bias) and underreliance (algorithm aversion) are real, context-dependent, and harmful. The direction depends on task type, user expertise, AI transparency, and whether the user has witnessed AI failures. Guo et al. (2024) provided the first formal framework for measuring the gap between observed and optimal reliance. Current interventions (cognitive forcing, XAI, confidence displays) produce modest effects with non-trivial cognitive costs.

2. Complementary performance. The aspiration that human-AI teams outperform either alone is achieved in a minority of studied conditions (Vaccaro et al., 2024). The main bottleneck is human error detection: users cannot reliably identify when the AI is wrong (Bansal et al., 2021). Most studies use classification tasks with known ground truth, limiting generalization to real decisions under genuine uncertainty.

3. Effective uncertainty communication. LLMs communicate uncertainty poorly: they are reluctant to express uncertainty (RLHF suppresses hedging), their expressed confidence doesn't track accuracy, and users — especially non-experts — rely on surface cues (fluency, detail) that are uncorrelated with reliability. Hybrid verbal-numeric formats (Budescu et al., 2014) are evidence-supported but rarely implemented.

The paper does not report new empirical results but provides a useful organizing framework and identifies the three challenges as connected rather than independent: achieving appropriate reliance requires both effective uncertainty communication and realistic expectations about complementarity.
