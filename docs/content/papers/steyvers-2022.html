Bayesian Models of Human-AI Integration

Steyvers, Tejeda, Kerrigan & Smyth · , 2022

AI Agents

Developed a formal Bayesian model of human-AI integration: both the human and the AI are modeled as noisy estimators of the true state, each with a specific reliability level. The optimal integration rule weights each source's estimate by its relative reliability — the more reliable source gets more weight. Under the model's assumptions, the combined estimate is always at least as good as the better source alone.

The model makes specific predictions about when human-AI teams should outperform either source: when the human and AI have uncorrelated error patterns (they make different mistakes on different instances) and when both sources have known, calibrated reliability. Under these conditions, the optimal combination outperforms either source, and the magnitude of the improvement depends on the correlation between errors — lower correlation means larger gains from combination.

The model fitted observed human-AI team performance reasonably well in several datasets. However, the assumptions are demanding and rarely met in practice: (1) AI and human errors are often correlated — both struggle with the same hard cases. (2) Reliability is not known to the human — they must infer it, which they typically do poorly (see Bansal et al., 2021). (3) The human's weight-of-advice depends on factors beyond reliability (fluency, confidence, social dynamics) that the model does not capture. The model is useful as a normative benchmark (what would optimal integration look like?) but overestimates the achievability of complementarity in real deployments.
