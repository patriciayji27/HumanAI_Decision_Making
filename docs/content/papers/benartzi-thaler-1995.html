Myopic Loss Aversion and the Equity Premium Puzzle

Benartzi & Thaler · , 1995

Temporal Dynamics Decision Quality

The specific insight

Stocks lose money in about 40% of months but only about 30% of years. That 10-percentage-point gap, combined with 2× loss aversion, accounts for the entire equity premium puzzle — why stocks return ~6% more than bonds annually.

A loss-averse investor (λ≈2) evaluating monthly sees near-coin-flip odds of loss, demanding a huge premium. The same investor evaluating annually sees losses only 30% of the time — a much more tolerable rate. The evaluation frequency alone changes the optimal portfolio.

Experimental confirmation

Gneezy & Potters (1997, N=72): subjects seeing outcomes every round invested 50.8%; every 3 rounds invested 67.3%. Haigh & List (2005): professional CBOT traders showed the same pattern — expertise does not eliminate myopic loss aversion.

Calibration caveat: with lower λ estimates (Walasek et al. 2024: λ≈1.31), the mechanism still operates but explains less of the premium.
