Uncertainty Displays Using Quantile Dotplots or CDFs Improve Transit Decision-Making

Fernandes, Walls, Munson, Hullman & Kay · CHI 2018

Uncertainty Communication Sampling & Experience

The specific contribution

Compared uncertainty displays for bus arrival time predictions in a realistic decision task (how early to leave home to catch a bus with high probability). Formats tested: 95% CI error bars, density plots, quantile dotplots, and CDFs. Both quantile dotplots and CDFs led to substantially better decisions than error bars — participants set departure times that more accurately reflected the underlying arrival time distribution.

Key results

FormatDecision qualityWhy
95% CI (error bars)Poor — participants treated interval endpoints as hard boundariesDeterministic construal: intervals misread as "the range within which outcomes always fall"
Density plotMixed — better than CI but area estimation is inaccurateArea-under-curve estimation is cognitively difficult
Quantile dotplotBest — improved probability estimation accuracyConverts probability estimation to counting (more natural)
CDFBest — tied with dotplotsAllows direct tail probability reading on y-axis

The paper provides empirical support for the theoretical argument in Kay et al. (2016): frequency-like representations (dotplots) reduce the abstraction required for probability estimation by letting users count outcomes rather than integrate areas.

Qualifications

Transit decision domain (bus arrival times) — a familiar, low-stakes context that may inflate performance relative to medical or financial uncertainty. Online study with MTurk workers. CDFs and dotplots both require brief onboarding for unfamiliar users; the study provided instructions. Long-term performance across novel tasks is untested.
