Mixed Debated

Are Probabilities Overweighted or Underweighted When Rare Outcomes Are Experienced (Rarely)?

Ungemach, Chater & Stewart · Psychological Science, 2009

Sampling & Experience Decision Quality

The specific contribution

Challenged whether the description-experience gap reflects different probability weighting or simply sampling error. When participants sample a rare event very infrequently in their sample (as they often do), they form incorrect estimates of its probability — and when these estimated probabilities are used in a standard prospect theory model, the observed choices are consistent with overweighting, not underweighting. The underweighting finding may be an artifact of information, not a difference in cognitive processing.

The decomposition

When Ungemach et al. controlled for the probability the participant actually experienced (based on the outcomes they sampled), much of the description-experience gap disappeared. The residual gap — which remained after controlling for sample information — was smaller than the original Hertwig et al. (2004) reports suggested.

FactorContribution to apparent underweighting
Sampling error (didn't observe rare event)Large — accounts for majority of the gap
Recency weightingModerate — recent samples weighted more
Genuine processing differenceSmaller than original papers suggested

Qualifications

The debate remains open. Fox & Hadar (2006) made a similar argument; Hertwig & Erev (2009) responded that a genuine residual gap exists after controlling for sampling. Camilleri & Newell (2011) found the gap persisted in a study that enforced equal information between description and experience conditions. The mechanism — pure sampling error vs. genuine processing difference — is still contested.
