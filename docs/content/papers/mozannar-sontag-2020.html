Strong Canonical

Consistent Estimators for Learning to Defer to an Expert

Mozannar & Sontag · ICML 2020

AI Agents

The specific contribution

Foundational formalization of the learning to defer problem: jointly learning a classifier and a deferral rule that routes individual instances either to the AI model or to a human expert, to maximize system-wide performance. Unlike prior work that used the model's own confidence as a deferral proxy, Mozannar & Sontag proposed a consistent surrogate loss function — grounded in cost-sensitive learning — that directly optimizes the joint human-AI system objective.

Key result

The surrogate loss LCEα generalizes the cross-entropy loss and is shown to be consistent (minimizing the surrogate minimizes the true system loss in the limit of infinite data). The approach learns a deferral policy that complements the human expert: the model focuses on regions where the human is unreliable, and defers otherwise.

Why it matters for this archive

The "adaptive delegation" protocol described in the AI Agents stream — route easy cases to AI, hard ones to humans — requires exactly this kind of deferral mechanism. Mozannar & Sontag provide the theoretical baseline that subsequent work (Okati et al. 2021, Verma & Nalisnick 2022) has extended. The key unsolved problem: expert behavior at deployment time may differ from the training distribution — a direct instance of the same out-of-distribution problem AI models face.
