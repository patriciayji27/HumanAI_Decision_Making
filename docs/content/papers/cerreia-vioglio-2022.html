Making Decisions under Model Misspecification

Cerreia-Vioglio, Hansen, Maccheroni & Marinacci · arXiv, 2022

Risk & Ambiguity Decision Quality

The specific contribution

Extends max-min ambiguity aversion (Gilboa-Schmeidler) to settings where the decision maker not only faces multiple candidate models but also knows all of those models are approximations — misspecified. Prior ambiguity theory assumed decision makers were uncertain which model was correct; this paper assumes the decision maker suspects that none of the candidate models is fully correct, and incorporates this misspecification concern into the decision criterion.

The decision criterion

The resulting criterion generalizes Hansen-Sargent multiplier preferences (which penalize deviations from a single reference model) to allow for a structured set of candidate models, each potentially misspecified. The "misspecification fear" parameter λ controls how much weight to put on model-violating scenarios — scenarios outside the support of any candidate model. When λ=0, the criterion collapses to max-min EU; as λ→∞, it approaches expected utility with respect to the best-fit model.

Why it matters for AI

AI models are, by construction, misspecified approximations of the data-generating process. A decision framework that acknowledges model misspecification — rather than treating the model's confidence as a calibrated probability — is more appropriate for AI-assisted decisions than standard EU or even standard ambiguity models. The parameter λ could, in principle, be estimated from the history of model errors, providing a quantified "misspecification premium" that should inflate displayed uncertainty.

Qualifications

Axiomatic theory paper with limited empirical application so far. Identification of the misspecification fear parameter from behavioral data is an open problem. Primarily relevant to economists and decision theorists; has not yet been translated into practical interface design guidelines.
