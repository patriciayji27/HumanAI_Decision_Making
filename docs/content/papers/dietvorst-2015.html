Algorithm Aversion

Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey · , 2015

AI Agents

The specific finding

Participants who saw a forecasting model make errors used it 48.6% of the time vs. 75.0% who didn't see errors (p < .001). The asymmetry: participants tolerated identical error magnitudes from human forecasters.

The modification fix

ConditionUsage rate
Cannot modify36%
Modify by ≤2 pts64%
Modify by ≤5 pts71%
Modify by ≤10 pts72%

Adjustments were small enough to barely affect accuracy. Users needed the perception of control, not real control. This is the most directly actionable finding for AI adoption: editable AI outputs restore adoption at near-zero accuracy cost.

Complication: Logg et al. (2019) found the reverse — overreliance — for objective tasks. Horowitz & Kahn (2024, N=9,000) found a Dunning-Kruger curve: low AI literacy → slight aversion, medium → overreliance, high → calibrated.
