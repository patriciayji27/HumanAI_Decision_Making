Uncertainty Forecasts Improve Decisions

Joslyn & LeClerc · , 2012

Uncertainty Communication

Compared protective action decisions when participants received point forecasts ("the temperature will be 31°F") versus range forecasts ("the temperature will be between 28°F and 35°F") in a winter weather scenario where the decision was whether to protect vulnerable plants from frost.

Participants who received range forecasts made better decisions: they were more likely to take protective action when the forecast range included dangerous temperatures (below 32°F), even when the point forecast (the central estimate) was above freezing. The range forecast made the possibility of dangerous cold explicitly visible, while the point forecast suppressed this information.

This is a case where communicating uncertainty is itself the intervention. A point forecast of 31°F gives the user one number to react to. A range forecast of 28°F to 35°F gives the user the information that freezing temperatures are within the forecast's uncertainty range — triggering appropriate protective action. The uncertain forecast outperformed the precise-but-possibly-wrong point estimate because it preserved decision-relevant information about tail risk that the point estimate discarded.

The finding extends to AI systems: presenting a single AI prediction ("the model says 73% chance of X") discards uncertainty information that may be critical for the user's decision. Presenting a range or distribution preserves it. Whether this translates from weather contexts to AI-assisted decisions has not been directly tested but the mechanism (making tail risk visible) is general.
