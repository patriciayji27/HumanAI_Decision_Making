Adaptive Learning from Sequential Choice

Hills, Noguchi & Gibbert · Psychological Science, 2013

Sampling & Experience Attention & Salience

The specific contribution

Proposes that the direction of experience-based biases (underweighting vs. overweighting of rare events) depends on the statistical structure of outcomes in the natural sampling environment. Using a foraging framework, the paper argues that decision makers calibrate their sampling strategies to the statistics of the environment they encounter — and when those statistics match the environment in which heuristics evolved, the heuristics perform well; when they don't match, the same heuristics produce systematic errors.

The key argument

In most natural foraging environments, valuable rare events (prey, water sources) are positively autocorrelated over short time scales — if you found food here yesterday, there's a higher probability it's here today. Recency-weighting of recent outcomes is therefore an ecologically rational heuristic. The description-experience gap in the lab arises because lab gambles have artificially independent outcomes, creating a mismatch between the heuristic's origin environment and its current application.

Qualifications

Framework is largely theoretical with selective empirical support. The operationalization of "natural environment statistics" for non-foraging human decisions is underspecified — it's unclear how to measure what the relevant natural statistics are for financial or medical decisions. The ecological approach is an important corrective to pure lab-based conclusions, but generating precise quantitative predictions remains difficult.
