A meta-analysis of loss aversion in risky contexts

Walasek, Mullett & Stewart · , 2024

Decision Quality

Independently re-fitted cumulative prospect theory parameters to individual-level raw data from 17 published studies (the subset where authors shared their data). Unlike Brown et al. (2024), who compiled published λ estimates from 607 studies, Walasek et al. re-estimated all CPT parameters (α, λ, γ) from scratch using maximum likelihood estimation on each individual participant's choices.

The result: median λ = 1.31, with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval of [1.10, 1.53]. This is substantially below both the original Tversky & Kahneman (1992) estimate of 2.25 and the Brown et al. (2024) meta-analytic estimate of 1.96. The difference likely reflects that published estimates may embed authors' modeling decisions (choice of functional form, treatment of noise, inclusion criteria) that systematically inflate λ. When all parameters are re-fitted simultaneously to individual data, loss aversion is smaller.

The study also found considerable within-study heterogeneity: in most datasets, a substantial fraction of participants showed λ ≤ 1 (loss neutrality or loss seeking). The authors identified several methodological factors that influence estimates: asymmetric gain-loss ranges, the stimulus distribution, and the number of trials per participant all affect the recovered λ. The limited number of usable datasets (17) is itself informative — it reveals how few studies archive the raw choice data needed for independent re-analysis.
