The Description-Experience Gap in Risky Choice

Hertwig & Erev · , 2009

Sampling & Experience

Comprehensive review establishing the description-experience gap as a robust research program. Synthesized the finding across multiple labs, paradigms, and experimental designs: when probabilities are described (stated explicitly), rare events are overweighted in choices — consistent with prospect theory. When probabilities are learned from sequential sampling (drawing outcomes and learning the distribution through experience), rare events are underweighted.

The review clarified that the gap has two possible components: (1) A statistical component — with small samples, rare events are often not encountered, so behavior that looks like "underweighting" may simply be rational response to incomplete information. (2) A cognitive component — even after controlling for sample information, there may be a processing-mode difference, perhaps driven by recency weighting (recent samples are overweighted relative to older ones) or memory limitations (early samples are forgotten).

The review identified key moderators: the gap is largest when sample sizes are small (median ~15 draws in most studies), when rare events have probabilities below 15%, and when the sampling is self-terminated (participants choose how many samples to draw) rather than experimenter-determined. The mechanism debate (sampling error vs. processing mode) was identified as unresolved at the time and remains debated as of subsequent work (Fox & Hadar, 2006; Camilleri & Newell, 2011).
