Strong Canonical

Small Feedback-Based Decisions and Their Limited Correspondence with Description-Based Decisions

Barron & Erev · Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 2003

Sampling & Experience

The specific contribution

Original empirical documentation of the description-experience gap. In description-based tasks (participants see the full probability distribution), behavior follows prospect theory — rare events are overweighted. In experience-based tasks (participants discover outcomes by sampling), behavior is reversed — rare events are underweighted. The same participants, the same payoff structure, opposite behavior.

The reversal

Task typeRare event weightingPredicted by
Description (shown probabilities)Overweighted relative to objective probabilityProspect theory's probability weighting function
Experience (sample outcomes)Underweighted relative to objective probabilitySampling error + recency weighting of recent outcomes

The mechanism proposed: when sampling, participants who never observe a 1-in-10 event treat it as a 0-in-10 event. The underweighting of rare events is partly a consequence of the information actually received (sampling error) and partly a consequence of recency — more recent samples receive more weight in the evaluation.

Why it matters for AI

AI systems that present risks numerically (description mode) — "there is a 2% chance of this outcome" — will produce different user behavior than systems that let users observe outcomes over time (experience mode). Most AI interfaces operate in description mode. Users who interact with AI systems that rarely fail will underweight the failure rate, even if they are told the numerical failure probability. The design implication: narrative examples of failure cases may be more effective than numerical probability statements for calibrating user expectations about AI errors.
