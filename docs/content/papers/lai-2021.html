Towards a Science of Human-AI Decision Making: A Survey of Empirical Studies

Lai, Chen, Liao, Smith-Renner & Tan · arXiv, 2021

AI Agents

What it covers

Systematic review of 100+ empirical human-AI decision-making studies from 2018–2021, drawn from CHI, CSCW, FAccT, IUI, ACL, EMNLP, and NAACL. Inclusion criteria: must include human subject evaluations of a decision-making task focused on human decision outcomes (not just model accuracy).

Key gaps documented

DimensionFinding
Task coverageDominated by recidivism prediction, income classification, medical imaging — few studies on sequential, open-ended, or advice-taking tasks
Evaluation metricsMost studies measure accuracy and reliance rate; few measure calibration, complementarity, or long-term learning
AI assistance elementsConfidence scores and feature importance dominate; protocol design (timing, commit-first) severely underrepresented
ComparabilityNo common benchmark tasks or metrics — results across studies cannot be directly compared

The paper argues the field needs common frameworks for study design — without them, individual results cannot be aggregated into general principles. A key recommendation: measure both trust calibration and decision quality, since Zhang et al. (2020) showed they can dissociate.

Qualifications

Survey bounded to 2018–2021. The field has grown rapidly since; many of the gaps identified (protocol design, long-term effects, LLM-specific reliance) are now active research areas. NLP venue focus may underrepresent HCI-only work published in venues like UIST or IUI.
