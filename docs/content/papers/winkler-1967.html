The Assessment of Prior Distributions in Bayesian Analysis

Winkler · Journal of the American Statistical Association, 1967

Belief Elicitation

The specific contribution

First systematic treatment of probability elicitation as a methodological problem distinct from statistical estimation. Winkler showed that different elicitation procedures applied to the same expert produce different prior distributions — that the procedure itself influences the distribution obtained. This is the original empirical documentation of what this archive calls the "constructive elicitation" problem.

Key observations

Winkler compared methods for eliciting continuous prior distributions from experts: the fractile method (ask for quantiles of the distribution), the histogram method (ask the expert to distribute probability mass across bins), and the "equivalent prior sample" method (ask the expert to express beliefs as if they were equivalent to observing a hypothetical sample). Each method produced numerically different distributions from the same expert about the same quantity.

The paper established the key design principles that became standard in expert elicitation: decompose complex quantities into simpler components, use multiple methods and check for consistency, and treat the elicitation process as a communication problem between the elicitor and the expert — not merely a measurement problem.

Legacy

Foundational for all subsequent expert elicitation research. The SHELF (Sheffield Elicitation Framework, O'Hagan et al. 2006) and virtually all structured elicitation protocols trace their methodological principles to Winkler's early work. The core problem he identified — that the method shapes the belief — remains unresolved and is increasingly relevant as AI systems elicit beliefs from users through conversational interfaces.
