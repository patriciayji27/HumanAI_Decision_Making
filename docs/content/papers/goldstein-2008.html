Distribution Display Changes Investment Behavior

Goldstein, Johnson & Sharpe · , 2008

Sampling & Experience Uncertainty Communication

Tested whether showing people the full distribution of possible retirement outcomes — rather than just the expected return — changes their investment behavior. Participants chose portfolio allocations (stocks vs. bonds) for a retirement savings plan.

In the control condition, participants saw summary statistics: expected return and standard deviation for each asset class. In the treatment condition, participants saw histograms showing the distribution of possible portfolio values at retirement — including the left tail (the worst outcomes) and the right tail (the best outcomes).

The result: participants who saw the full distribution became substantially more conservative, increasing their bond allocation and decreasing stock allocation. The effect was driven by the distribution making tail risk tangible: seeing a histogram that shows "there's a visible bar representing scenarios where your retirement savings are worth only $150,000 instead of the expected $500,000" makes the downside risk concrete in a way that "standard deviation = 18%" does not.

The finding demonstrates that information granularity is a causal design variable: the same underlying data produces different behavior depending on whether you show the mean, the mean ± interval, or the full distribution. More granular displays are more informative but also more cognitively demanding, and the additional information they convey (tail risk) may trigger loss aversion in ways that summary statistics do not.
