Algorithm Appreciation

Logg, Minson & Moore · , 2019

AI Agents

Documented algorithm appreciation — the flip side of algorithm aversion (Dietvorst et al., 2015). Across multiple experiments, participants weighted advice more heavily when told it came from a statistical algorithm than from a human advisor. The effect was specific to tasks perceived as objective (estimating quantities like populations, weights, or distances) rather than subjective (predicting preferences or emotions).

The design: participants received advice from either "an algorithm" or "a person" (actually the same advice) and then made a final estimate. The weight of advice (how much the participant shifted toward the advice) was higher for algorithmic advice than human advice across most tasks. The effect persisted when participants knew the algorithm's accuracy level and when they had experience with both advice sources.

The interaction with task type is the key finding: for objective tasks, people show appreciation (over-rely on algorithmic advice). For subjective tasks, people show aversion or indifference. Castelo, Bos & Lehmann (2019) extended this, showing task type is the primary moderator of the aversion-appreciation continuum. The practical implication: the relevant question for AI system design is not "will users trust the AI?" but "will users calibrate their trust to the AI's actual domain competence?" — which depends on whether the user perceives the task as objective or subjective, not just on the AI's accuracy.
