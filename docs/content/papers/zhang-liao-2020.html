Effect of Confidence and Explanation on Accuracy and Trust Calibration in AI-Assisted Decision Making

Zhang, Liao & Bellamy · FAT* 2020

AI Agents

The specific contribution

Two experiments (N≈180 each) in which crowdworkers made income prediction judgments with and without AI confidence scores or local LIME explanations. The study's central distinction: trust calibration (whether humans correctly adjust their reliance case-by-case) vs. joint accuracy (whether calibrated trust actually improves final decisions).

Key findings

InterventionEffect on trust calibrationEffect on joint accuracy
AI confidence scoreImproved — users showed higher agreement with AI when AI was high-confidenceNo improvement over baseline
Local LIME explanationMixed — increased general trust but not case-by-case calibrationNo improvement

The critical finding: confidence scores successfully calibrated trust in the sense of shifting reliance when the model was more certain, but this did not translate into better joint decision accuracy. The reason: complementarity requires humans to bring unique correct judgments when the model errs — but in this domain, humans and AI had comparable accuracy and errors were not sufficiently independent to enable complementarity.

This paper established the trust calibration vs. accuracy gap as a core research problem in human-AI decision making and became one of the most-cited papers in the field (800+ citations).

Qualifications

Income prediction task (UCI Adult dataset) with crowdworkers — low ecological validity for professional decision contexts. AI and human accuracy were roughly matched by design; domains where AI is substantially better or worse may show different dynamics. LIME explanations used as-is; better explanation designs might produce different results.
