Interpreting IPCC Probabilistic Statements Around the World

Budescu, Broomell & Por · , 2009

Uncertainty Communication

Tested whether readers correctly interpret the IPCC's standardized verbal probability scale — a system where "very likely" is defined as >90% probability, "likely" as >66%, "unlikely" as <33%, and "very unlikely" as <10%. The IPCC adopted this scale in its assessment reports specifically to reduce ambiguity in communicating scientific confidence about climate projections.

Participants from multiple countries read IPCC report excerpts containing verbal probability terms and indicated the numeric probability they believed was intended. The key finding: approximately 40% of the time, readers assigned a lower probability to "very likely" than the IPCC intended (>90%). "Likely" (intended >66%) was interpreted as <66% about 25% of the time. The misinterpretation was systematic: readers consistently compressed verbal terms toward 50%, interpreting high-probability terms as less extreme than intended and low-probability terms as less extreme than intended.

The error was not uniformly distributed across readers: numeracy, scientific literacy, and familiarity with probability concepts moderated the effect, with less numerate readers showing larger misinterpretation. However, even scientifically literate readers showed meaningful levels of misinterpretation — the standardized verbal scale reduced variability relative to unstandardized verbal communication but did not eliminate it.
