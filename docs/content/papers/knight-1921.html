Risk, Uncertainty and Profit

Knight · Houghton Mifflin, 1921

Risk & Ambiguity

The specific contribution

First formal distinction between risk and uncertainty in economics. Knight defined risk as situations where outcomes are uncertain but the probability distribution is known or can be calculated (rolling a die, actuarial mortality rates). Uncertainty — later called Knightian uncertainty — refers to situations where no objective probability can be assigned because the situation is novel, unique, or too complex to enumerate outcomes (starting a new business, developing a new technology).

The distinction and its implications

ConceptKnight's termModern equivalentExample
Known probability distributionRiskAleatory uncertaintyInsurance actuarial tables; casino games
Unknown probability distributionUncertaintyEpistemic/Knightian uncertainty; ambiguityNovel market entry; AI model out-of-distribution error rates

Knight's core argument: genuine entrepreneurial profit exists because of irreducible uncertainty, not risk. If all outcomes could be probabilities, competition would drive profit to zero. It is the uninsurable nature of true uncertainty that creates the opportunity for profit.

Why it matters for AI

Most AI systems present themselves as risk engines — outputting probability scores as if the underlying distribution were known. But AI models deployed on out-of-distribution data face Knightian uncertainty: the model's confidence score reflects its training distribution, not the actual probability of error in the deployment context. Treating AI confidence as "risk" (calculable) when it is actually "uncertainty" (unknowable) is the precise failure mode that drives user overreliance in novel deployment contexts.
