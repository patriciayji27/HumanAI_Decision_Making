Cumulative Prospect Theory

Tversky & Kahneman · , 1992

Decision Quality

Extended prospect theory from simple gambles (two or three outcomes) to prospects with any number of outcomes using rank-dependent probability weighting. In the original 1979 version, probability weighting was applied to each outcome's probability independently, which could produce violations of first-order stochastic dominance — a serious theoretical flaw. Cumulative prospect theory (CPT) fixes this by applying the weighting function to cumulative probabilities (the probability of getting at least x), ensuring that a prospect that is better in every state is never rated worse.

CPT provided the parametric forms used in most quantitative applications of prospect theory: the value function v(x) = xα for gains with α ≈ 0.88, and v(x) = −λ(−x)β for losses with β ≈ 0.88 and λ ≈ 2.25. The probability weighting function was parameterized as w(p) = pγ / (pγ + (1−p)γ)1/γ with γ ≈ 0.61 for gains and γ ≈ 0.69 for losses. These specific numbers — α, β, λ, γ — are what most empirical work uses when fitting prospect theory to choice data, estimating risk preferences, or calibrating behavioral models.

The estimates were derived from certainty equivalents for 56 gambles, assessed with 25 graduate students. The parameter estimates vary considerably across subsequent studies: see Brown et al. (2024) and Walasek et al. (2024) for the range of λ estimates, and Stott (2006) for comparisons of alternative functional forms.
