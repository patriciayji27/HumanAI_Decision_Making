Error Bar Misinterpretation

Belia, Fidler, Williams & Cumming · , 2005

Uncertainty Communication

Asked published researchers — authors of papers in Psychological Science and other journals — to look at figures showing two group means with error bars and judge whether the means were significantly different. The task is one that researchers routinely perform when reading papers.

Performance was poor. Many researchers could not correctly assess whether overlapping error bars indicated a statistically significant difference. A major source of confusion: different types of error bars look identical but carry different information. Standard error (SE) bars, standard deviation (SD) bars, and 95% confidence interval (CI) bars are all drawn as vertical lines extending from a point, but: (1) non-overlapping SE bars can indicate significance while overlapping SE bars may also indicate significance (because the threshold for significance depends on the comparison, not just the overlap), (2) SD bars describe sample variability, not inferential uncertainty, (3) 95% CI bars can overlap substantially and still indicate a significant difference.

Without explicit labeling of which type of bar is displayed — which many published figures omit — the reader cannot determine what the bars mean. The study revealed that this ambiguity causes errors even among researchers who have published papers using error bars, suggesting the format is inherently misleading for visual inference tasks.
