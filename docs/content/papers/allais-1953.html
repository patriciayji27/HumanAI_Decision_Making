Strong Canonical

Le comportement de l'homme rationnel devant le risque (The Allais Paradox)

Allais · Econometrica, 1953

Decision Quality

The specific contribution

The original demonstration that expected utility theory fails as a descriptive model. Allais presented participants with two choice problems. Most people choose 1A (certain 1M) over 1B (10M with p=0.10, 1M with p=0.89, nothing with p=0.01), but then choose 2B (10M with p=0.10, nothing with p=0.90) over 2A (1M with p=0.11, nothing with p=0.89). This pair of choices violates the independence axiom of expected utility: the two problems are identical except for the addition of an 89% chance of 1M in both options — and EU theory requires this common consequence to be irrelevant.

Why it matters

The Allais Paradox set the normative vs. descriptive agenda for decision theory. It showed that rational agents, as defined by von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms, are systematically violated by real preferences — and that the violations are not random errors but structured. The certainty effect (people overweight certain outcomes relative to equally favorable but probabilistic ones) is one of the most replicated findings in all of behavioral science. Prospect theory's probability weighting function is designed specifically to accommodate this paradox.

Qualifications

Original evidence was from surveys with hypothetical outcomes. Incentive-compatible replications have confirmed the core pattern, though effect sizes vary with stakes and population. The violation is strongest when one option is certain; it attenuates when both options are probabilistic.
