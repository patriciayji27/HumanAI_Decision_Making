Loss aversion is not robust: A re-meta-analysis

Yechiam, Zeif & Ashby · , 2025

Decision Quality

Re-meta-analyzed Brown et al.'s (2024) comprehensive dataset of 607 loss aversion estimates, introducing two moderators drawn from the methodological critique literature: (1) gain-loss symmetry — whether the range of possible gains and losses was symmetric or asymmetric in the experimental stimuli, and (2) ordering — whether gains and losses were presented in ordered (ascending/descending) or randomized sequences.

From the original dataset, 84 papers (163 estimates, n = 149,218 participants) could be categorized on these moderators. The key finding: for studies with symmetric gains and losses and no ordering, the estimated loss aversion parameter was λ ≈ 1.07, not significantly different from 1.0 (i.e., no loss aversion). For studies with asymmetric gains/losses or ordered presentation, loss aversion remained significant and close to the canonical values.

The interpretation: stimulus design artifacts — presenting a wider range of gains than losses, or ordering stimuli by magnitude — may inflate measured loss aversion. When these features are controlled, loss aversion largely disappears in the aggregate data. This does not mean loss aversion is nonexistent in all individuals or all contexts, but it suggests that the population-average estimate is substantially influenced by experimental design choices. The paper has generated active debate and its conclusions depend on accepting its moderator categorizations as valid.
