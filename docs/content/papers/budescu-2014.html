Effective Communication of Uncertainty in the IPCC Reports

Budescu, Por, Broomell & Smithson · , 2014

Uncertainty Communication

Follow-up to Budescu et al. (2009), testing whether adding explicit numeric ranges alongside verbal terms would reduce misinterpretation of IPCC probability language. The intervention was simple: instead of writing "very likely," the report would read "very likely [>90%]."

The hybrid verbal-numeric format reduced misinterpretation by roughly half compared to verbal-only. Participants who saw "very likely [>90%]" assigned probabilities much closer to the IPCC's intended meaning than those who saw "very likely" alone. The benefit was particularly large for low-numeracy readers, who struggled most with the verbal-only format.

The finding is directly applicable to AI system design: LLMs communicate uncertainty almost exclusively through verbal hedging ("I'm fairly confident," "this is likely but not certain"). Appending numeric indicators — even approximate ones — should reduce the between-user variability documented by Wallsten et al. (1986). The intervention requires no model changes, only output formatting changes, making it one of the lowest-cost, highest-evidence improvements available for AI uncertainty communication.
