Bending the Automation Bias Curve

Horowitz & Kahn · , 2024

AI Agents

Large-scale pre-registered experiment across 9 countries (N=9,000) testing how AI background knowledge, trust in AI, and task characteristics interact to predict automation bias (overreliance on AI) vs. algorithm aversion (underreliance).

The key finding is a Dunning-Kruger-type pattern for AI knowledge: (1) Those with the lowest level of experience with AI were slightly more likely to be algorithm-averse — they didn't trust the AI enough. (2) Those with intermediate AI knowledge showed the highest rates of automation bias — they trusted the AI too much. (3) Those with the highest AI knowledge showed the most calibrated reliance — they neither systematically over- nor under-relied.

Additional moderators: (1) Task difficulty — harder tasks produced more reliance on AI advice. (2) Described AI competence — telling participants the AI was "highly competent" increased reliance, while describing it as "less competent" decreased reliance, regardless of actual performance. (3) Overall AI trust (a dispositional trait) predicted reliance independently of knowledge and task features.

The study is the largest cross-national test of automation bias in human-AI decision-making, using nationally representative samples rather than convenience samples. The Dunning-Kruger finding suggests that AI literacy interventions may be more important than interface design interventions for calibrating human-AI reliance — though the two are not mutually exclusive.
